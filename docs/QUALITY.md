# Quality

## Required Checks

For every source, shader, configuration or dependency change:

1. `pnpm lint`, `pnpm format:check`, `pnpm test`.
2. `pnpm build`.
3. `pnpm capture` with the affected presets and views. Look at the images and the contact sheet. Console errors, including shader compile errors, make it exit non-zero.

CI ([`.github/workflows/ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/ci.yml)) runs lint, formatting, tests and the build on Node 22.

## Captures

`pnpm capture [--presets a,b|all] [--views beauty,iris-clay] [--samples 64] [--width 1600] [--height 1000] [--dpr 1] [--out captures] [--query key=value] [--hash debug] [--bench <frames>] [--no-build] [--chrome <path>] [--timeout 600]`

The script:
1. builds the app into a temporary directory and serves it with `vite preview`;
2. drives headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol ([`scripts/lib/chrome.mjs`](../scripts/lib/chrome.mjs));
3. loads `?preset=…&gui=0&photo=<samples>`, plus `view=` and the extra query (the eye starts frozen);
4. waits until the accumulation converges, and saves `window.__EYE__.capture()`.

It writes into `captures/`, which is git-ignored:
- the PNGs;
- `report.json`, with the time per shot, the frame intervals, the compile and skin bake times, and the console errors;
- `contact-sheet.html` / `.png`, with the captures above the reference photographs (each labeled with the photographs its preset is tuned on).

`--hash debug` loads the pages with `#debug`: the skin evaluates its relief per pixel instead of reading it baked.

`--bench <frames>` also renders that many interactive frames per shot with `measureSync()`, and reports the mean, median, minimum and maximum milliseconds and the compile time.

To check an arbitrary framing, pass a state string: `--query state=$(node -e "process.stdout.write(Buffer.from(JSON.stringify({camera:{azimuth:60}})).toString('base64url'))")`.

## Comparing Captures

`pnpm compare --baseline <dir> --candidate <dir> [--out <dir>] [--psnr 50] [--within 2] [--share 0.999] [--bias 0.5] [--cluster 2]`

[`scripts/compare.mjs`](../scripts/compare.mjs) pairs the PNGs of two capture folders by their relative path, typically the same shots from two builds (render the baseline from a copy of the tree, `git archive <ref>`, never by editing tracked files). Per pair, `compareImages()` ([`scripts/lib/png.mjs`](../scripts/lib/png.mjs)) measures, on the largest channel difference per pixel (8-bit):
- the PSNR, the largest difference and the shares of pixels over 0, 1, 2, 4 and 16;
- the tile bias: the largest mean signed luminance difference over a 16×16 tile, where a systematic shift (a flipped or offset lookup) shows while noise averages out;
- the outliers (over 16), in 4-connected clusters: isolated pixels are noise, blobs are not.

A pair passes with PSNR ≥ `--psnr`, at least `--share` of its pixels within `--within`, no tile bias over `--bias` and no cluster larger than `--cluster` pixels; a baseline image the candidate lacks fails. It writes `compare-report.json`, the difference images (|Δ| × 16 and × 64, the signed luminance difference, the tile biases, the outlier mask) and `contact-sheet.html` with the worst pairs first, and exits non-zero when a pair fails.

## Visual Checks

- **Presets**: each one against its reference photographs on the contact sheet. Compare iris structure, color and collarette, the catchlight shapes, the lid and lash reflections in the cornea, the tear meniscus, and the vessels.
- **Iris**:

## Performance

- **Interactive frames** set the budget. The adaptive pixel ratio (down to `quality.minPixelRatio`) and the cheaper interactive tier keep the eye responsive. Photo mode renders one full-quality sample per frame.
- **Budget** (Apple M1, headless Chrome, 3440×1440 at DPR 1, a living eye, October 2026): about 33 ms per interactive frame at the start framing (`camera.distance` 285, f/17) and 47 ms zoomed in (180 mm), against 52 and 83 ms before the split frames, the coarse eyeball mesh and the depth of field's tiles. Kept frames (photo mode) are whole: about 33 ms for the default preset at 1600×1000. The cornea and iris are the largest part (the field's evaluations near the surface: the end of the march, the normal, the occlusion and the soft shadow, with the fibers the largest part), then the skin. Baking the skin's relief (outside `#debug`) took a still eye's interactive frame from about 53 to 42 ms at the start framing and from 72 to 67 ms zoomed in (fastest batches, the two modes alternated in one page with `setSkinBaked()`). The bake itself takes about 0.35 s of GPU work since the stars of furrows (0.15 s before them), timed with `?debug=1` by clearing `__EYE_APP__.lids.reliefInputs` and `__EYE_APP__.lids.relief.key`, calling `__EYE_APP__.bakeSkin()` and reading a pixel of the lid texture back. The cost scales with the pixel count.
- **Load and start-up** (October 2026): `dist/` is about 1.9 MB, the main script about 1.1 MB (310 KB gzipped, 340 KB with the schema and the population priors it shares with the tools; most of it GLSL, minified by the build); the development tools, the panel (with Tweakpane), the mesh tree, the presets and GSDevTools are chunks of their own, which a plain page never downloads ([`docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`](DEVELOPMENT.md#url-flags)). From navigation to the first frame, in headless Chrome with vsync on the M1 (1600×1000, a new profile each time), the page takes about 1.0 s (1.4 s before the compile overlapped the first rebuild; the very first load of a new build 1.7 s), its longest task about 0.8 s (1.25 s), with 32 programs compiled before the first frame (34 before the compile overlapped the first rebuild; counted again on October 23, with the Sharingan's bake and the shockwave) and none after, a Sharingan shown and the shockwave included.
- **Second pass** (October 2026, Apple M1, headless Chrome, 3440×1440, each change timed against the one before in the same page, its draw or pass alone, a hundred alternated batches; the image identical but for a level in 8 bits on a few pixels):
  - the eyeball's draw: 4.45 → 4.0 ms at the start framing, 15.85 → 15.4 ms zoomed in, fresh frames (the march's last evaluation reused by the shading, the defocus blur only where the micro striations ask for it, fewer values held across the trace);
  - the skin's draw (lids and back shell): 4.85 → 4.55 ms fresh and 3.3 → 2.75 ms per sample at the start framing, 6.6 → 6.05 and 4.75 → 3.65 ms zoomed in (the lit emitters' irradiance per vertex, the wet mirror only near the margin);
  - the composite: 2.4 → 2.0 ms at the start framing (its early outs); a shown photo sample from the 16th on: 14.2 → 11.8 ms at the start framing, 14.4 → 10.9 ms zoomed in (effects and grain in one pass);
  - the main thread of a living frame (the app's `renderSample()`): 3.4–3.7 → 0.9–1.2 ms (median), the light shafts' cookie baked on the GPU;
  - a photo of 64 samples, with vsync, until the GPU is done: 1.06 → 0.69 s at 1600×1000 and 1.51 → 0.95 s at 3440×1440 at the start framing, 1.58 → 1.38 s and 3.46 → 3.09 s zoomed in (shown every 150 ms, several samples per frame).
  - Measured and left out: the march stepping back to the last safe distance exactly, skipping the emitters below the tangent plane and tracing the iris only where its weight passes 1e-3 under the limbus (none faster); the skin's albedo in one `exp2` (no faster); split frames (they change the image, above); a deferred cap shading (the cap drawn as a full-screen triangle: 0 to 0.65 ms off its draw at most, before the stencil prepass it would need); the noise lattice in a table ([`docs/ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md#future-work)).
- **Dust and haze**: timed in isolation (each pass repeated with a synchronous read, headless Chrome on the M1, 3440×1440, the start framing), the dust pass takes about 0.13 ms (a thousand motes, a few pixels each; blurs past 60 px at 1440 px of height are dropped) and the haze is within the noise of the depth of field's passes (about 0.1 ms). A whole living frame does not change measurably (medians 50.6 ms with them, 51.1 ms without). Over a converged still eye, the drifting dust redraws the composite, the dust and the effects at most 30 times a second.
- **Light shafts and soft coverages**: timed the same way (in batches of ten, 3440×1440, seven blades), the shafts in the air add about half a millisecond to the depth of field's passes on average (the runs swing by a millisecond either way); each soft coverage costs about a millisecond (the shafts' with their caster draw), and is redrawn only when the light, `backdrop.softness` or the lids change. A whole living frame does not change measurably.
- **Sharingan** (October 23, Apple M1, headless Chrome, 1600×1000 zoomed in, `measureSync(60)` medians, alternated in one page): a shown pattern costs the frame nothing measurable (about 33 ms with it or without, within half a millisecond), reading its baked maps (also once they became fields, read twice during a morph: about 1 ms more in a run where the page itself drifted by 5); evaluating the shapes per pixel instead, as first written, cost up to 6 ms, and still 2.5 to 4.5 ms with each shape's bounds and early outs (Shisui's, then Madara's and Sasuke's eternal ones). A bake (1024² half-float, once a pattern) is a single draw at the press. Off, the eyeball's programs only test its amount.
- **Measurement**:
  - Use `--bench` or `window.__EYE__.measureSync()` to compare builds on the same machine, at the same size and DPR.
  - Close every other page that renders WebGL (a tab with the app in `pnpm dev`): it shares the GPU and makes timings swing by a third. Render a few frames between synchronous reads and keep the fastest batch; alternate the builds under comparison.
  - `measure()` reads GPU timer queries where available (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, Chrome only). On ANGLE-Metal they are inflated and cumulative: a query around one draw reports the time from the start of the command buffer, so successive draws' differences give their costs.

## Known Limitations

- **Timing**: frame timings in headless captures are not representative (no vsync, frame rate limit off). Wall-clock measurements swing widely on a loaded machine; repeat them and compare medians.
- **Safari**: the scene targets Chrome and Safari on Apple Silicon. The capture script runs Chrome only, so Safari needs a manual check.
- **Sound**: headless Chrome plays into no device, so a probe can count sounds and read their levels but not judge them; the sounds need a listen.
