# Quality

## Required Checks

For every source, shader, configuration or dependency change:

1. `pnpm lint`, `pnpm format:check`, `pnpm test`.
2. `pnpm build`.
3. `pnpm capture` with the affected presets and views. Look at the images and the contact sheet. Console errors, including shader compile errors, make it exit non-zero.

CI ([`.github/workflows/ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/ci.yml)) runs lint, formatting, tests and the build on Node 22.

## Captures

`pnpm capture [--presets a,b|all] [--views beauty,iris-clay] [--samples 64] [--width 1600] [--height 1000] [--dpr 1] [--out captures] [--query key=value] [--bench <frames>] [--no-build] [--chrome <path>] [--timeout 600]`

The script:
1. builds the app into a temporary directory and serves it with `vite preview`;
2. drives headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol ([`scripts/lib/chrome.mjs`](../scripts/lib/chrome.mjs));
3. loads `?preset=…&gui=0&photo=<samples>`, plus `view=` and the extra query (the eye starts frozen);
4. waits until the accumulation converges, and saves `window.__EYE__.capture()`.

It writes into `captures/`, which is git-ignored:
- the PNGs;
- `report.json`, with the time per shot, the frame intervals and the console errors;
- `contact-sheet.html` / `.png`, with the captures above the reference photographs (each labeled with the photographs its preset is tuned on).

`--bench <frames>` also renders that many interactive frames per shot with `measureSync()`, and reports the mean, median, minimum and maximum milliseconds and the compile time.

To check an arbitrary framing, pass a state string: `--query state=$(node -e "process.stdout.write(Buffer.from(JSON.stringify({camera:{azimuth:60}})).toString('base64url'))")`.

## Visual Checks

- **Presets**: each one against its reference photographs on the contact sheet. Compare iris structure, color and collarette, the catchlight shapes, the lid and lash reflections in the cornea, the tear meniscus, and the vessels.
- **Iris**:
  - `?view=iris-clay` shows the relief without color: bridging fibers, undercut crypts, the ruff.
  - `?view=iris-steps` should stay blue over the iris; red marks pixels at the march limit.
- **Optics**: orbit a still eye (any reference preset) to grazing angles. The iris stays visible through the cornea and the limbus fades smoothly. `?preset=refraction-checker` shows the refraction as a polar grid.
- **Dilation**: sweep `iris.pupilDiameter` from 2 to 7 mm on a still eye. The collarette follows the pupil and the crypts stretch without re-baking.
- **Lids**: on a still eye, sweep `animation.pitch` over ±25°. The lids follow the gaze (up, the upper lid rises) without opening a gap on the globe.
- **Background**: tilt the camera far (`camera.azimuth` 70, `camera.elevation` 45). The backdrop past the back shell is a darker skin tone, with no trace of earlier frames.
- **Back shell**: zoomed out (`camera.distance` 480), from either side and from above and below (`camera.azimuth` ±80, `camera.elevation` ±40 and ±60), on a still eye and on one looking up and down (`animation.pitch` ±25): no part of the globe, the conjunctiva or the palpebral conjunctiva shows behind the face, and the shell follows the face's profile. The flat band runs along the whole edge with a sharp edge on both sides and no crack against the skin; the shell is darker skin, without freckles or veins, with no lattice or streaks in its relief, also close up (`camera.distance` 240). From the front and in the canthi's pockets (the **Canthi and margin** and **Closed pockets** framings) the image does not change: what the shell cuts or moves never shows.
- **Face**: against `references/desired-output.png`, framed the same way (`camera.azimuth` 8, `camera.distance` 470, a still eye with `animation.yaw` 8): the eye sits in a soft hollow under a broad brow, the lids flow into the face without a seam, the lower lid into the cheek, the side of the nose rises at the right, and nothing in the face has an edge. Zoom out (`camera.distance` 520, `camera.fNumber` 22) and orbit (`camera.azimuth` ±40 and ±80, `camera.elevation` ±40): no thin creases running out of the canthi, no protruding brow, nose or cheek.
- **Canthi and margin**: close up (`camera.distance` 175, `camera.fNumber` 32, `camera.targetX` ±11): the medial canthus is a narrow U with a small caruncle in it and the plica beside it, both shadowed by the baked occlusion, the lateral one an acute rounded corner where the upper lid laps over the lower one; the margin flows into the skin without a step.
- **Closed pockets**: from the default framing (azimuth −52°), from above and below (`camera.elevation` ±40) and from the nose side (`camera.azimuth` 20 to 60), the pockets behind both canthi show the caruncle and the palpebral conjunctiva darkening into the sac, never the backdrop. To make a leak obvious, set the backdrop to a vivid color from the console with `?debug=1`: `__EYE_APP__.backdropRadiance = () => [0, 4, 0]`, then `__EYE__.setParams({})`.
- **Lid shape**: against `references/Four-view anatomical eye sculpt reference.png` from the front, three-quarters, the side and below (`camera.distance` 300, `camera.fNumber` 22): the upper lid is a convex band with its fold above the pretarsal show, the lower lid a lower band that flows into the cheek, the brow stands over the upper lid in profile. Set `lids.closure` to 0.5 and 1: the upper lid slides down over the globe, over the cornea's bulge, and meets the lower one; the face does not move.
- **Skin**: with a deep focus (`camera.fNumber` 32 through `--query state=…`), and with `--views normals`, the lids show the crease and its fold, the pretarsal roll and the furrows (with `skin.wrinkles` raised from its default 0, the shaded crow's feet too), with no seam at the canthi and no repeating pattern. A wider framing (`camera.distance` 330) shows the whole periocular region.
- **Split frames**: on a living eye, with the panel, turn `quality.splitShading` off and on: the image does not change (the blurred lashes stay continuous strands, not dots), only the frame rate. Compare a frame against a whole one through `window.__EYE_APP__` (`post.accumulation.passthrough`, `renderSample(null)`, then the canvas).
- **Living eye and transitions** (`pnpm dev`, with `#debug` for the panel's presets): the default eye looks around straight ahead in smooth shifts, its pupil easing with each look. Switch presets and press **Random eye**: the camera, the colors and the pupil glide; seeds and counts switch at the start. **Zoom out** glides to the whole figure, **Zoom in** closer than the start; in a portrait window (or a phone held upright) Zoom out goes farther, so the figure fits across, and Zoom in back to the start. Captures start frozen and do not show either.
- **HUD** (`pnpm dev`, a desktop window and phone-sized ones in both orientations; captures hide it): the central dashed circle stays inside the frame, cut around the flat square controls. The selected tone is full size, the others smaller and faded; the whole shape scales, with a flat swatch and no glass. Brackets face the reticle, start hidden and farther apart, move inward on hover/focus, and remain visible on the selected tone and reached zoom endpoint. The two segmented rings each show two arcs and linear markers, keep their zoom-out sizes, rotate in opposite directions over the full zoom (inner 22.5°, outer 45°) and stay inside/outside the central circle throughout. The square zoom marker follows +/− glides and the wheel; dragging it along the arc zooms without snapping while held, even near the endpoints; pulling beyond either stop extends the marker and camera with progressively greater resistance to a finite maximum, and release near or beyond a stop glides back to it; it stays centered on the circumference at the snapped endpoints too, turns tangent to the track and briefly pulses on click. As the marker approaches either button, only its + or − symbol progressively scales and fades to zero at overlap; it returns when the marker moves away, including beyond the stop during elastic dragging. Check both symbols on desktop and phones, mid-track, the endpoints and after resize. Check release in mid-track (no snap), reversal back inside from overshoot, interrupted returns, pointer cancellation and lost capture, both endpoints, both phone orientations and `zoom.elasticity` at zero. Check the keyboard slider controls, full hit areas on smaller inactive tones and reduced-motion preferences. The shuffle icon and credit line remain present; no glass filters or asset-loading errors should appear.


## Performance

- **Interactive frames** set the budget. The adaptive pixel ratio (down to `quality.minPixelRatio`) and the cheaper interactive tier keep the eye responsive. Photo mode renders one full-quality sample per frame.
- **Budget** (Apple M1, headless Chrome, 3440×1440 at DPR 1, a living eye, October 2026): about 33 ms per interactive frame at the start framing (`camera.distance` 285, f/17) and 47 ms zoomed in (180 mm), against 52 and 83 ms before the split frames, the coarse eyeball mesh and the depth of field's tiles. Kept frames (photo mode) are whole: about 33 ms for the default preset at 1600×1000. The cornea and iris are the largest part (the field's evaluations near the surface: the end of the march, the normal, the occlusion and the soft shadow, with the fibers the largest part), then the skin. The cost scales with the pixel count.
- **Measurement**:
  - Use `--bench` or `window.__EYE__.measureSync()` to compare builds on the same machine, at the same size and DPR.
  - Close every other page that renders WebGL (a tab with the app in `pnpm dev`): it shares the GPU and makes timings swing by a third. Render a few frames between synchronous reads and keep the fastest batch; alternate the builds under comparison.
  - `measure()` reads GPU timer queries where available (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`, Chrome only). On ANGLE-Metal they are inflated and cumulative: a query around one draw reports the time from the start of the command buffer, so successive draws' differences give their costs.

## Known Limitations

- **Timing**: frame timings in headless captures are not representative (no vsync, frame rate limit off). Wall-clock measurements swing widely on a loaded machine; repeat them and compare medians.
- **One-sample captures**: `start()` renders a frame before `compileAsync()` and restarts the accumulation after it, so during the compile `--samples 1` already reads as converged and can capture that first frame, before the eye takes its pose. Wait for `getCompileMs()` to be set, or capture two samples or more.
- **Safari**: the scene targets Chrome and Safari on Apple Silicon. The capture script runs Chrome only, so Safari needs a manual check.
